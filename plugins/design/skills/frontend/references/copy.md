# Copy

Say what it is and what it does, in words a stranger understands in five seconds. Every sentence is
one you would say out loud to a customer. No metaphors, poetry, invented terms or ad wording.

The examples below are in English; write the page in the brief's language and apply the same rules
there. The tells list carries English and Portuguese (pt-BR) entries; for another language, look
for that language's equivalents of each tell.

## Five rules

1. **The h1 names the category, and for a brand nobody knows, the buyer or the place,** in 2 to 8
   words: "Payroll app for restaurants", "Bakery on Mill Street, Leith". A game: its name and
   genre. Never a riddle, a command, a mood or a slogan.
2. **The first screen holds one hard fact:** a number, a place, a price or a date.
3. **Every heading has a noun that names a thing:** the product, a part of it, the place, a section
   noun (prices, rooms, menu, opening hours, questions, getting here). One brand line a page at
   most, never the h1, with a fact in the line under it.
4. **Buttons are a verb plus what the visitor gets,** 1 to 5 words (Book a table, Start a free
   trial, See prices). Never "Learn more", "Read more", "Discover", "Explore" or "Click here".
5. **Few actions, each with one wording:** at most 2 in the hero (1 on a place), 10 different
   labels a page at most. The main action reads the same in the nav, the hero, the close and the
   phone bar.

## Plain versions

| Vague | Plain |
|---|---|
| "Every shift, sorted" | Shift planning for restaurant teams |
| "Where the city climbs" | Climbing gym with 180 routes in the old rail yard |
| "Come climb" | Day pass 14 €, shoes included |
| "A second read of every word" | AI proofreader for book publishers |

## Sizes and shapes

- **Hero sub-line:** how it works and the main benefit, 15 to 30 words. A place: the fact, 80
  characters at most, with today's hours.
- **Section headings:** 1 to 6 words, two lines at most; a noun phrase, "we do X for Y", or a verb
  with the thing it acts on. Never a bare command, a question (FAQ entries aside), a colon heading
  or a line that only makes sense after reading the page.
- **Card titles:** a verb and an object ("Warns about double shifts"), one line.
- **Under a heading:** 10 to 40 words. A card body 25 words at most, with one concrete detail. No
  paragraph over five lines at 1440.
- **Words per screen** stay low (about 35 to 50 outside tech, 60 to 80 on tech); a page about 900
  words at most, the footer aside. Long copy goes to sub-pages.
- **Numbers** sit in sentences with their unit ("4.5 hours a week").
- **Form words:** a label says what goes in; an error names the field and what to type; the sent
  line names what was sent ("Your table request is in. We reply within a day.").

## Nothing invented

- Prices, address, hours and dates come from the brief. Only two things may be assumed when the
  brief lacks them: a coined brand name, and on a place plausible hours and prices for the trade,
  uneven and in the local currency. Each assumed value carries `data-assumed` on its element and is
  listed in the report.
- No invented reviews, ratings, quotes, testimonials, statistics, customer counts, customer names,
  client logos, awards or press lines. Without proof, a strip of plain facts from the brief, or
  nothing.
- No "sample", "illustrative", placeholder, square brackets, lorem ipsum, TODO, or link to `#`.
  Write the real words, or leave the element out. "Coming soon" only for what the brief says is
  coming.
- A coined name is not a dictionary word, not a business that exists and not one models keep
  coining (Kestrel, Halcyon, Lumen, Nimbus, Acme, Apex, Zenith, Vertex). Person names come only
  from the brief.
- Each fact has one value and one wording everywhere it appears.

## Tells of model-written copy

One tell proves little; clusters and repetition are the signal. Every ban needs a positive move
beside it, or the nearest substitute appears: the fix is almost always a fact from the brief in
place of the adjective.

**Words that stand in for a fact** (a word that is literal for the trade stays: a climbing harness,
bespoke tailoring):

- English: delve, tapestry, testament to, realm, embark, beacon, myriad, plethora, meticulous,
  elevate, unlock, unleash, empower, harness, supercharge, seamless, effortless, cutting-edge,
  revolutionary, transformative, holistic, next-level, world-class, best-in-class,
  game-changing, state-of-the-art, synergy, leverage, reimagine, powerful, robust, nestled,
  unparalleled.
- Portuguese: potencialize, eleve, desbloqueie, alavanque, revolucionário, transformador,
  disruptivo, de ponta, de outro nível, de excelência, sem complicação, sem esforço, robusto,
  poderoso, completo, inovador, jornada (outside travel), ecossistema.

**Stock lines:** "In today's fast-paced world", "Unlock the power of", "The future of X is here",
"Say goodbye to", "Whether you're A or B", "Join thousands of", "Trusted by teams worldwide",
"Ready to transform your X?", "X made simple", "at your fingertips", "in just a few clicks", "It's
not just X, it's Y"; in Portuguese "No mundo acelerado de hoje", "Diga adeus a", "Seja você A ou
B", "Junte-se a milhares", "O futuro de X chegou", "a um clique", "na palma da sua mão", "não é só
X, é Y", "no coração de", "onde X encontra Y".

**Shapes:**

- the -ing rider tacked onto a fact (", ensuring a smooth start" / ", garantindo uma experiência
  tranquila");
- the question answered at once ("The result? Three hours back.") and the colon reveal;
- em dashes everywhere; exclamation marks in body copy; emoji as bullets;
- residue: "Certainly!", "[Company Name]", template tokens, markdown on the rendered page.

**Second-generation tells** (they look like good copy):

- **The house voice:** warm, wry, modest, a little literary, for every client. Choose a register
  per client instead.
- **Invented specifics as decoration** ("6:12 a.m., from the window of Room 9", "Batch No. 047").
- **The strip of figures:** three or four big numbers over one-word labels under the hero.
- **Two-fragment headings** ("Twelve rooms. Three ways to stay."): once a page at most.
- **Contrast frames and their substitutes** ("not X but Y", "rather than", "instead of", "without
  the"): two a page in all.
- **Virtue words about the client:** honest, calm, quiet, genuinely, truly, simply, thoughtful,
  carefully, intentional (honesto, calmo, de verdade, simplesmente, cuidadoso, intencional). Show
  it with a fact.
- **The dry closer** on the end of every paragraph; **the footer sign-off** ("Made in X, fired at
  1,260 degrees").
- **The origin template** ("In 2019, two friends found an old hardware store...") with no real
  story in the brief.
- **Polished testimonials:** always three, each with a number and a punchline.
- **The default cast** of names: Priya, Marcus, Dana, Maya, Theo, Nora, Isla, Sarah J.
- **The section template:** eyebrow, claim heading, short lede and three cards, every section.
- **Proof figures:** "3x faster", "10,000+ teams", "98% satisfied", round and unsourced.
- **Perfect evenness:** every card the same length, every paragraph the same arc.
- **Process talk:** "This section shows", "Below, you'll find", "Here are 3 reasons".
- **A stale year** in the copyright line.

## Over-correction

| Rule applied | Habit it breeds | Counter |
|---|---|---|
| No em dashes | comma splices, full stops only | colons, brackets, semicolons where natural |
| No "not X but Y" | "rather than", "instead of" | count them together; state the positive |
| No hype words | calm vocabulary: quietly, steady, honest | a fact in place of any adjective |
| Avoid threes | everything in pairs | list as many items as there are |
| Be specific | invented numbers and anecdotes | the brief's facts, or a plainer line |
| Short sentences | a drumbeat of fragments | one sentence a section may run long |
| Add personality | a joke in every section | humour spent in the microcopy only |

**Not tells:** perfect grammar, plain "is" and "has", a list of three when there are three things,
copy the client wrote, selling on a sales page (the test is whether it stands on facts).

## Contacts and links

- Print what the brief gives exactly as given; invent nothing (house rule 9).
- No map iframe loads with the page; a "Get directions" link opens a map search for the street the
  brief gives.
- Links go where their label says; never "here", never two labels to one target.
- A newsletter is asked once, in or just above the footer, never in a modal.
- Credits: one short footer line naming the sources used.
